A PRACTICAL STARTING POINT / CHANTAL VAN KEMPEN

How do you make learning active and practical?

Start with what participants need to do in practice. Give them a situation to try, space to reflect and a concrete next step for their own work. The format follows the learning need.

Understanding an insight is different from putting it into practice.

An inspiring session can spark curiosity. To take it further, people also need something to investigate or try. Which decision feels difficult? Which conversation keeps getting postponed? What collaboration needs practice? Linking a learning goal to a recognisable situation makes the relevance of an experience clear.

Four starting questions

  1. Frame the learning goal as an action, rather than just a topic.
  2. Design a safe practice situation with choices, feedback and room to experiment.
  3. Make time to reflect: what happened and which assumptions shaped it?
  4. Let participants choose a small experiment and arrange a follow-up.
