A PRACTICAL STARTING POINT / CHANTAL VAN KEMPEN

How do you help people break familiar patterns?

First make a pattern recognisable. Explore what it offers and what it gets in the way of. Then choose a small experiment that opens up a different perspective without deciding the outcome in advance.

Personal development does not always begin with more explanation.

Sometimes you understand your behaviour, but a different environment or activity helps you notice something new. Storytelling, play and reflection can offer a way in. Participants do not all need to have the same experience. They need room for their own choices and for deciding what they want to share.

Four starting questions

  1. Choose a specific pattern, such as automatically agreeing or repeatedly postponing an idea.
  2. Explore when the pattern helps and when it limits you.
  3. Design a small activity that invites a different choice.
  4. Reflect without forcing a correct outcome, then choose a next step.
